How the circuit works

Above is a basic motor controlling circuit used in many applications. The circuit operates by a momentary type control switch, which returns to the middle position after being released.

Operation starts by moving the control switch to the “ON” position. Current then flows from the “plus” terminal, through “Fuse 1” to the coil of “Relay R” and through “Fuse 2” to the “minus” terminal. The coil becomes energized and the relay closes its contacts R1 and R2.

After the contacts close, the motor starts running, fed from the “plus” terminal through “Fuse 1” to the motor, through contact R2 and “Fuse 2” to the “minus” terminal.

After the control switch is released and returns to the middle position, relay R remains energized from the “plus” terminal through “Fuse 1” to the relay coil, the 120-ohm resistance, the closed contact R1, and through “Fuse 2” to the “minus” terminal. Even though the resistor cuts out part of the current to the relay coil, it continues to hold the contacts closed, because it takes much less energy to sustain the relay in the energized position than to energize it in the first place.
