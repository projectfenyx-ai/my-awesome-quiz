Wire Size and Resistance

Most people think of a wire as having zero resistance, but in actual practice all wires have resistance. In industrial installations, where total length of wiring can reach hundreds of miles, resistance of wires must be properly calculated.

Four factors set a wire’s resistance

  • The DIAMETER of the wire
  • The MATERIAL the wire is made of
  • The LENGTH of the wire
  • The TEMPERATURE of the wire

Area

Diameter of wires is measured in mils, where 1 mil = 1/1000 inch. Cross-sectional area of wire is measured in circular mils.

Circular mil area = diameter in mils, squared.
Squaring a number means taking that number and multiplying by itself.
Example 1Diameter 12 thousandths of an inch (0.012) = 12 mils.
12 × 12 = 144 CM
Example 2Diameter 0.045 inch.
45 × 45 = 2025 CM

Stranded wire

Area of one strand × number of strands.
Example18 strands, each 0.015 inch in diameter.
One strand = 225 CM
225 × 18 = 4,050 CM

Large wires are usually stranded to make them easier to bend.

The larger the diameter of a wire, the less resistance it has and the more current it can carry.

Current flowing through a wire is very similar to water flowing through a pipe. A large pipe can carry more water at a specific pressure than a small pipe. A large wire can carry more current at a specific voltage than a small wire.

Material

A standard measurement used for finding the resistance of wire is the mil-foot: a piece of wire one circular mil in diameter and one foot long.

If the resistance of a mil-foot of different types of wire is known, a formula can find the resistance of any type, size, and length of wire:

R = K × L / CM
K
ohms per mil-foot of the wire
L
length of the wire, in feet
CM
circular mil area of the wire

Practical problems

Problem 1 What is the resistance of a piece of #18 AWG copper wire 400 feet long?

Solution

Formula: R = K × L / CM

K for copper (Table 1) = 10.4.
Area of #18 wire (Table 2) = 1624 CM.

R = 10.4 × 400 / 1624
R = 4160 / 1624
R = 2.56 ohms
Problem 2 What is the resistance of a piece of #12 AWG aluminium wire 250 feet long?

Solution

R = K × L / CM
R = 17 × 250 / 6530
R = 4250 / 6530
R = 0.6508 ohms
